DROUGHT STRESS: 24-nt siRNA vs CHH METHYLATION
Final analysis
===============================================

DATASETS

Small RNA:
GSE26356
4-week-old Arabidopsis, Col gl1 RD29:LUC, shoots.
Control vs drought to 40-50% relative water content.
One library per condition.

DNA methylation:
GSE94075, mitotic drought experiment.
4-week-old Col-0, whole rosette.
Control vs nine days water deprivation.
Three biological replicates per condition.

The datasets are biologically similar in age, aerial tissue and drought
treatment but are from different studies, genotypes and drought protocols.
Results therefore describe cross-study spatial correspondence and must not
be interpreted as direct causality.

baseline CHH methylation:

Thus the apparently strong tail enrichment is almost entirely explained
by the starting genomic state.


FINAL INTERPRETATION

Drought was associated with a global reduction in 24-nt siRNA abundance
and a reproducible increase in CHH methylation.

Unadjusted analyses suggested an inverse spatial relationship between
changes in 24-nt siRNA abundance and CHH methylation, including an
approximately twofold enrichment of reproducible CHH gains among loci
with the strongest 24-nt siRNA losses.

However, this apparent inverse relationship was almost entirely explained
by baseline genomic state. Regions with characteristic high baseline
24-nt siRNA and CHH activity preferentially contributed to both responses.

After controlling for baseline 24-nt siRNA abundance and baseline CHH
methylation, locus-specific correspondence between drought-associated
changes in the two molecular layers was very weak.

Therefore, the available cross-study drought datasets provide little
evidence that locus-specific drought-induced changes in 24-nt siRNA
abundance directly track corresponding changes in CHH methylation.

This conclusion does not imply that 24-nt siRNAs do not regulate CHH
methylation through RdDM. Positive-control analyses (ALY1 and DDC)
demonstrate that the analytical pipeline readily detects spatial
siRNA-CHH coupling when a strong coupled signal is present.
